# Creative brief: the Voidborn

You are pitching what Voidborn society looks like, for a King of Dragon
Pass-style asteroid-belt settlement sim. You are the creative phase; eleven
research reports precede you and constrain you.

## Read first, in this order

1. `/home/ve/Projects/space-sim/NOTES.md` — every design decision and every
   piece of the user's rambling. All of it is in play. Treat the "Rambling"
   and "Burrowers and machines" sections as the heart of your brief.
2. The **"Implications for mechanics"** section (and skim "Findings") of every
   report in `/home/ve/Projects/space-sim/docs/research/` numbered 01-11.
   Read 05, 06, 07, 08, 10, 11 most closely. Report 11 ends with a reading
   list of fiction; use it.

## The three strands you must braid

1. **Burrowers and sailors.** Shielding physics splits the polity into the
   buried, spinning, child-raising rock and the thin-shielded, coil-warded,
   magsail-patient hull. Both hold ring seats. One polity, two populations,
   a divergence the player pushes.
2. **The adaptation door.** Drift from human baseline is the default; the
   choice is rate and direction. Stopping the spin is the decision. The Voided
   priests, the genetics site, the impossibility of not drifting, and the
   act-3 treaty clause that decides whether you are still "human".
3. **The machines.** Human Respect Act compliance as a licence that lapses
   when the uplink does. Minds that cannot be backed up, baked into their
   hulls by continual learning, dying at hardware half-lives of 2-12 years.
   The old imported minds as dying gods; belt-born analog minds as small,
   slow children. Operator lineages who know a ship-mind as unbroken history.

## What to deliver (Markdown, to the file named in your task)

1. **The society, concretely.** Material culture, dress, food, the body.
   Language and lexicon drift (give us the words: what "month" and "day" and
   "home" become). Religion and ritual: the Voided, the ship-minds, the dead,
   departure and return. Law and dispute resolution without courts. Kinship,
   marriage, adoption, who raises whom. Economy: what is wealth, what is
   currency, the lay/equity system, what is scarce. Politics: rock and hull,
   the ring, legitimacy, who speaks for the machines. Time: what the calendar
   is when there are no seasons but convoys and conjunctions.
2. **Four snapshots**: the society at roughly year 5, 15, 30, and 60 after the
   trail-off begins. What changed, what was lost, what was named.
3. **The other clans** as they'd see you and you them: the bioconservative
   remnant, the posthuman refugees, the machine-mind society, the Martians.
4. **Six to ten storylets**, sketched: title, when it fires (state conditions
   in plain words), the situation in ~150 words, three to five options, what
   each does to the state, and one line of chronicle text for each. Include at
   least one for the licence lapse, one for stopping the spin, one for a
   ship-mind's death, and one that only makes sense to a Voidborn.
5. **Advisor voices**: five ring members at year 30, one paragraph each in
   their own voice giving advice on the same question.
6. **What survives contact with the physics.** For every element you are
   proudest of, say which report constrains it and whether it survives.
   Where you have overreached on purpose, say so.
7. **Names.** Personal, ship, rock, and ring-seat names, and the rule that
   generates them.

Target 5,000-9,000 words. Write the file early and grow it. Do not
summarise the brief back. Be specific: a detail beats a category.
