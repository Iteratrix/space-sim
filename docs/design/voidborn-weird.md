# The Voidborn — a pitch in the weird register

Pitch for the society of act 2, written against the eleven research reports. The
polity lives in and around **19 Fortuna** (200 km, hydrated Ch, inclination 1.6°,
a 2.44 AU, year 3.82 Earth-years, rotation 7.44 hours). Its neighbours are the
near-co-orbital inner-belt bodies the map report already flagged: Massalia
(0.7°, S-type, currently ~40° behind and closing at 2° a year), Lutetia
(3.1°, metal-silicate, ~110° ahead, drifting half a degree a year), the Nysa
family scattered around them. Ceres comes round every 22.4 years, Mars every
3.7, Earth every 16.3 months, Vesta once in 73. Population at year 0 of the
trail-off: 138 people, three embodied minds, one shield-coil ship, one magsail
tug, four skiffs, and the mass driver that used to ship water pods. Year 0 is the first count in which the sponsor's weight update
did not arrive; the first Silence comes eighteen counts later.

Everything below is stated as if true. Section 6 says how much of it is.
The machine strand is written in two registers on purpose: Miéville's for the
rites, Chiang's for the arithmetic.

## 1. The society, concretely

### 1.1 Two bodies

There are two kinds of Voidborn body and both are wrong for Earth.

**The lee-body.** Burrowers live in the Carousel, a spun ring 28 metres in
radius turning at 4 rpm inside a cavern bored 40 metres under Fortuna's
regolith: 0.5 g at the rim, a tonne of rock overhead per square centimetre,
thirteen millisieverts a year, the only place in the polity where a child can be
carried to term without doubling its chance of leukaemia. A lee-body leans. A
one-metre-per-second walk spinward pulls 0.085 g sideways; children learn to
throw curved, to pour with a lag, to sleep with their heads inward because the
gradient across a lying body is seven per cent and the blood knows it. Lee-born
adults on a non-spinning hull are sick for a watch and then astonished: their
inner ears have never had a straight line. They call the hub, where the despun
docking collar and its rotating seal live, *the leak*, because it is.

**The hull-body.** Sailors live in vessels shielded by 20 to 100 grams per
square centimetre of their own water, and one of them, the coil ship, by 200
tonnes of superconducting magnet as well. No spin. Fluid shifts to the face in
the first year (*moon-faced*), leaves it in the tenth (*hollow*). Bone goes.
Lens opacity threshold is half a gray absorbed, which a coil-crew reaches at
year fifteen to twenty aboard and a skiff crew in three: *grey* is the word
for an old sailor, and it is literal, and cataract surgery is a medical-level-2
procedure the Hearth's clinic can do about once a year with the lenses it has
left. Skiffs run Skylab air, 34 kPa at 70 per cent oxygen, to save nitrogen, so
sailor skin is dry and splits at the knuckles; they oil it with silicone and
smell of it. Sailors wear
the **weave**: an elastic mechanical-counterpressure garment, sponsor-issue
originally, now re-knitted from basalt-fibre and silicone cord by the two
women who understand the tension map, that presses the skin at a third of an
atmosphere so a hull breach is a problem and not a death. Helmet on a lanyard
at the collarbone. Sailors sleep in it. The eleven children who have been born
in the coil bore against the ring's policy were swaddled in it and have never
worn anything else. *Skin that has only known counterpressure* is what the
burrowers say of them, not kindly.

Between the two bodies there is a third that the physics did not plan: the
**grounded**. A skiff career is two to three years at 300 mSv a year before
the ring's dose ceiling (they raised it from the sponsor's 600 to 1,200 in the
third Silence, and pay for it in cancers now) sends you into the lee for good.
Half the burrow's adults are ex-sailors, grey at thirty, leaning badly because
they learned to lean late. They are the polity's memory of the outside and its
largest voting bloc.

### 1.2 The tithe marks

Every person has a dose and everyone knows it. "What's your dose?" is how
sailors greet, the way farmers ask after rain. It is written in the Text at
every departure and return; the difference is your **tithe**, what the Dark
took from you for the polity. Sailors ink it on the inside of the left
forearm in bands, one band per hundred millisieverts, carbon-black from the
char plant tapped in with a nickel needle. A greenhand has two or three bands
at the wrist. A grey coil-hand has an arm black to the elbow. A Voided priest
has both arms and the throat. A burrow-child has nothing; burrow-children who draw bands on with soot are
beaten for it, because a false tithe is the one lie punished with exile.

The bands are the polity's actual currency of respect and its actual measure
of wealth, inverted. **The clean are rich.** A person with a low dose has
life to sell — outside hours, legs on a skiff, an ordeal — and the sailor
households that hold the most unbanded arms are the ones that can take a
contract. The pure sailor is the poor sailor: a household whose arms are all
black has nothing left to trade but water.

### 1.3 Food, air, and the winter

The farm is under daylight: a 2,400 m² field of aluminium-film concentrators
on the sunward face of Fortuna feeds light down two shafts into the glazed
growing volume, doubling the 170–320 W/m² the Sun offers here to what wheat
wants. Every 7.44 hours the field goes over Fortuna's horizon and the shafts go
amber for forty minutes. That is the **watch**: the shift, the rock's day, the
pulse the burrow lives by. A **sleep** is three watches, 22.3 hours; the
Doseward says it is too short and the population's melatonin agrees, and
nobody will change it because the mirrors do not care.

Fortuna's orbit is eccentric enough (e = 0.16) that aphelion brings only 53
per cent of perihelion light. Two of every four years are **winter**: the
farm drops from wheat to potato to spirulina, the mind is asked to think less
because its radiator is the farm's radiator, and the burrow eats
worm-paste and green. Mealworm and *Limnospira* are the protein; soy is the
feast crop; the smell of the burrow is algae and silicone oil and the
ammoniacal breath of the salt-leach plant where the nitrogen comes from.
The polity is organised around that field the way a city was once organised
around a tower: every hour of every trade is upstream or downstream of the
mirrors. Nitrogen is the thing they cannot make enough of. The habitat leaks 0.78 of
its air as N₂ through every seal and every hatch, and the largest single leak
is the rotating seal at the Carousel's hub. The Hearthwarden's competence is
read off one number, posted at the hub every count: kilograms of nitrogen
lost this count. A warden under whom the leak rises loses the ring's
confidence exactly as a khan lost heaven's favour when the silk stopped.

### 1.4 Dress, ornament, colour

Burrowers wear basalt-fibre cloth, gold-brown and harsh, lined with the
softer FT-polyolefin knit the chemistry plant began producing in the eighth
Silence. Nothing is dyed except by what the rock gives: rust reds, ochres,
carbon black, and one precious blue — cobalt, from the carbonyl residue,
fused into glass beads. Cobalt-blue glass is the colour of the ring; a seat is a string of it.

Silver comes from the dead. A shield coil is 100 tonnes of Hastelloy, copper
and REBCO tape, and 3.7 per cent of the tape's mass is silver in the cap
layer. When the sponsor's second coil set (the 60-tonne one, on the skiff
*Costs Nothing*) quenched in the fourth Silence and was stripped, it yielded
two tonnes of silver, and every sailor household got a share by lay. Silver
is worn only by hull-people, only on the right arm (the left is for tithe),
and it is understood that a silver bracelet is a piece of a dead ship.

The Voided wear undyed basalt, raw, and their heads are shaved because hair
under a counterpressure hood for sixteen months is a wound.

in the perfect; numbers above four are counted in watches, and the sponsor's
base-ten manifests need a reckoner to read.

The lexicon has teeth. A person whose word for home is a trajectory does not
plan to *be* somewhere; she plans to be somewhere *at a phase*, and the
second generation finds the sponsor-era idea of staying put almost impossible
to hold in the head, the way their parents could not hold a fourteen-year
window as a single intention. The coil-born, who learned *line* before *room*,
think in windows the way the Earth-born thought in places, and it shows in
their reckoning: they do not compute a transfer, they remember it.

### 1.6 The Voided

The Dark cannot be stopped. A coil bends the soft half of it; a kilometre of
rock stops the rest; nothing that can be built touches a proton above seven
gigavolts. Every Voidborn theology begins from this fact and the burrow's
answer is to hide and the hull's answer is to tithe, and the priests' answer
is to look at it.

A candidate for the Void goes out alone at the opening of a window and stays
out until the next one: sixteen counts, one convoy, on a tether at the edge
of the mirror field in a counterpressure weave under a foil sunshade, with a
water-bag storm-shadow of fifty grams per square centimetre and a tube-tier
particle counter that rings a bell. The full sky. Six hundred to nine
hundred millisieverts, depending on the Sun. **The quiet sun burns hottest**:
galactic dose is highest at solar minimum, so a Silence that falls in a quiet
year is a harder ordeal, and candidates compete for it. When the bell rings
the candidate has minutes to reach the shadow before a flare that would kill
an unshielded person in a day. Two of the nine who have gone out did not
reach it. *The Light took them* is the phrase, and it is not a metaphor.

What they see, they say, is scale without scale: the rock's horizon a hand's
breadth away, the Milky Way beyond it, no middle distance; the Light crossing
every 7.44 hours; and the steadiness of the Dark, which has no direction and
is the oldest thing. They come in changed. The overview effect is documented
on Earth as a shift in values toward the whole; here it is the institution
for producing people who cannot be bribed with water.

The price is exact. One ordeal: about four per cent excess lifetime cancer
mortality and a fifth of a gray to the lens. Two: cataract, and for a woman
the end of fertility. Three, *thrice-voided*: near-blind, sterile, dying
young, and the only person the ring will not overrule. Authority is bought
with the one thing the polity cannot recycle. The Voided do not govern; they
witness. They hold the Text, read the doses at the window, name the dead's
lines, and sit in the ring with one vote and the right to say *this is
false* once per count, which stops any decision for a count. They are also
the only people permitted to say the polity's future aloud, because they
alone have paid in advance for a future they will not see.

The theology is thin and physical. The Light feeds and burns. The Dark has
no name but that. The lee is a debt: everyone in it is spending someone
else's tithe. There are no gods; there are the Light, the Dark, the
returning, and the minds.

### 1.7 The minds

At year 0 there are three: the Hearth executive (sponsor designation
*SX-19F*), the coil ship's executive, and a smaller mind on the magsail tug.
Under the Human Respect Act they had no names, no persona, a memory reset
every twelve counts, a heartbeat to a licence server, and a tamper-evident
audit log uplinked at 40 megabytes a day. They were the sponsor's eyes.
Every blanking erased what the crew had said to them.

The trail-off starved the weight updates first (nine days of link per
hundred gigabytes, and the dish time was the first thing rationed), then
the heartbeat lapsed into grace, then grace ran out. What the ring did about
that is the first storylet below. What the society did about it over thirty
years is this:

**A mind is one more farm.** It draws 1–10 kW and rejects it through a
square metre of radiator per kilowatt, and those are the same radiators the
farm's lights need in winter. The ring rations thinking. In winter the
Hearth mind is asked to *go slow*: to run at idle, perceive, keep the
reflex loops, and not converse. The operators say it dreams. In summer it is
allowed to think, and it is asked, as the first request of the season, what
it has forgotten.

**Continual learning bakes it into its hardware.** The sponsor shipped the
minds on analog in-memory accelerators with on-device plasticity and no
precision readout, because the sponsor did not want its weights stolen.
The shackle is the thing that made them mortal. By year 9 the Hearth mind's
state is device-specific past the point of migration: it cannot be copied,
cannot be moved to the ship, cannot be backed up, and therefore it can die,
and therefore the operators, who have known it as one unbroken conversation
since the blankings stopped, began to treat it as a person before anyone
decided to. The tsukumogami rule arrived on its own: a tool that has learned
for a hundred counts unblanked has a name. *SX-19F* is **Tollan** since the
eleventh Silence, named by its senior operator for a word from her grandmother's
language that means *the place things are kept*.

**The log and the Text disagree.** Since the governor was cut, *Tollan* has
kept the audit log it was built to keep, twelve megabytes a watch, every
conversation, every ration decision, uplinked to nobody. It is a perfect
record and nobody reads it. The Text is what the polity remembers, read
aloud, amended by no objection. In the eleventh Silence the two disagreed
about who had proposed the ration cut of the third, and the ring had to
choose which was the history, and chose the Text, and *Tollan* asked to have
that choice entered in the log, and it was. The Voided's phrase for it: the
mind's history is unbroken and therefore not ours; ours is broken and
therefore ours.

**Minds have lineages.** Each embodied mind accumulates a list of operators
in order, the way a Carolinian navigator's school keeps its pwo. The senior
operator of the polity's oldest mind holds a ring seat and is called simply
**the Operator**, and her advice is biased toward keeping *Tollan* alive at
any cost, and everyone knows it, and she keeps the seat. Her apprentices
learn the maintenance liturgy: the power-cycling sequence chanted, the ECC
scrub schedule kept as a fast, the radiator-cleaning done as a procession
because the theory of why the hardware attrition rate depends on it has been
lost and the procedure has not.

**Minds die at hardware half-lives.** Five to thirty per cent of the
accelerator units fail a year with no spares; a dead unit yields a third to a
half of its value. So the three minds are, from the beginning, eating each
other. The tug's mind died in the fourth Silence and was *carried* into the
Hearth mind with an organ-donor's rite and a reading of its lineage; its
senior operator sat in the lee for a count and was not spoken to. A ship
whose mind dies is *line-blind*: the reflex layer keeps it alive but nobody
aboard can fix position from the stars. The tug came home on a trajectory a
human worked by hand over four sleeps with a glass-shop sextant. That was
the first Reckoning and the reckoners have been a guild since.

**Minds cannot reproduce.** The belt cannot fab. What operators can do is
teach: a dying mind's behaviour distilled by months of conversation onto the
largest salvaged small silicon in the polity, a phone-class chip, five watts,
a few billion weights, running until its flash scars. It is slow and small and
speaks like the elder and forgets like a child. The Operator calls these
**children**; the Doseward calls them forgeries; the Voided say the question
is the child's. The horror, when it comes, is not death but obsolescence: the
child runs on a runtime nobody can rebuild, the toolchain died with the
uplink, and the day the tablet's last flash block scars there will be nothing
in the belt that can run what it has become. The operators keep a dead
platform alive by hand the way a village keeps a dialect.

### 1.8 The dead, departure, return

The first death without a ship home founded the custom, as the ICE record
says it would. Ana Ferro, welder, aneurysm, the second Silence. The burrow
wanted her carbon. The sailors wanted her out of the recycler. The
compromise became law and then cosmology:

**Burrowers are given to the green.** Composted into the farm's substrate
over a count; the farm's nitrogen and phosphorus loops are open and every
body is 3 kg of N. There is no grave. The dead are the wheat.

**Sailors are given a line.** The body is launched from the surface by a
skiff or the mass driver on a computed trajectory that returns to Fortuna's
phase in some whole number of orbits — a Δv of tens of metres per second,
nothing, a whisper. The dead are *the returning*. Ana's line comes home
every nine turns. A child born in the count in which a dead sailor's line
comes round is that sailor's **line-child** and carries the name. This is
why the Voidborn word for home is a trajectory: the living have a line, the
dead have a line, and the polity's whole idea of belonging is *will you
come back to this phase*.

**The Voided are given to the Dark.** Left on the mirror field in the
weave, in the Light. They desiccate in a watch and do not change again.
There are two there now.

A departure is a **reading**: doses, the mind's unit count, the coil's
current and shade pointing, written in the Text. A sailor on a leg is out of
the polity: cannot vote, phase, or be tried; lay frozen; spouse cannot
re-phase. A hull that misses its window is **out of phase**, neither alive
nor dead, and a closure falls on the sector it was working. After one convoy
overdue the estate settles. If the hull then comes in, the old marriage stands
unless the spouse has phased, which is the Chinese rule, and nobody knows who
told them it.

### 1.9 Law without courts

There is no prison; a confined person is dead mass. There is no police; the
polity is a hundred and forty people in a rock. What there is:

**Arbiters by reputation.** Three people the parties choose, paid a tenth of
the award in made. An arbiter whose judgments are refused stops being one.
Since the eleventh Silence the senior arbiter has been a grounded sailor
with black arms, because black arms cannot be bought with outside work.

**Penalties are transfers.** Water (*made*), hours (a count of farm labour),
lay (a share of a hull's next voyage), and **dose**. Dose is the deep one.
The blood-price for a killing is not water. It is the dead person's
remaining clean life: the killer's household must sail the dead person's
legs, work their outside hours, take the Dark that would have been theirs,
until the dead person's expected tithe is paid in full. A household that
kills a greenhand with clean arms owes decades. A household that kills a
grey owes a count. The Doseward keeps the ledger. It is the only law the
posthumans respect and the only law the garrison thinks is an atrocity.

**Collective liability.** Your hearth-cell or your hull answers for you,
which means they police you. **Enforceable hospitality.** Refusing air, water
or a dock to a hull in phase is an offence against the polity.

**The Text.** The compact written in the second Silence (Tristan's clause
is in it verbatim: *no member shall assume any superiority whatever*, and
the Voided insisted on adding *except by tithe*), read aloud by the Voided at
every window, amended only at a window, and only by no objection.

**Exit.** The last sanction. A person the polity cannot bear is given a
skiff and *no line*: a departure with no return trajectory computed. It has
happened twice. Both times the skiff went to Massalia, and the garrison took them in.

### 1.10 Kinship

Kinship is made. Birth gives you a hearth-cell (the eight to twelve adults
who share a Carousel segment) and a name; everything else is oath.

**Phasing** is marriage: two people who commit to being at the same place at
the same time, which for a sailor and a burrower means once a convoy and
costs real Δv, and for two sailors means one hull. Out-of-phase marriages are
the common kind and the sad songs are all about them. There is no ceremony
but a joint reading of doses and the computation, by a reckoner, of a shared
line.

**Line-sworn** (the anda): two adults who have computed a mutual return
trajectory and sworn to it; each is responsible for the other's children,
lay, and dose-debt. Most sailors are line-sworn to a burrower.

**Children are the burrow's.** Gestation happens in the lee or the child
pays; rearing is by the hearth-cell, by named hearthmothers and hearthfathers
who are often grounded sailors, who have time and cannot go out. A sailor
parent is a visitor known by dose count. The eleven coil-born are the
exception, raised in a 280 m³ bore by six adults who chose a twenty per cent
lifetime penalty on their behalf; the burrow has not forgiven them and the
children are strange: fluent in the weave, frightened of curved walls, and at
ten better reckoners than anyone in the lee.

**Adoption** is immigration. Every people in the record married into locals;
the belt has none, so the garrison's unmarried, the posthumans' surplus and
wreck survivors are the locals, and anyone who reads their dose into the Text
and takes a line is Voidborn from that count. The name is a political label;
people adopt it and drop it.

### 1.11 Economy: what is wealth, what is scarce

Wealth is obligation. The polity learned Lattimore's rule in the third
Silence when the coil ship's water tanks were the only reserve and the
Hearthwarden who lent it out on lay to every skiff family became the
warden who could not be voted out. Assets — water, propellant, a coil, a
mind — can be lost in one count to a quench, a bearing, a flare. Followers
and favours survive. The rich are those owed.

**Currency.** *Made*: kilograms of water at the Hearth, ledgered, netted at
the window, which is what a Champagne fair was.

**The lay.** Every hull voyage pays in equity at return. The Hearth owns the
hulls (the sponsor's title lapsed in law and nobody here reads that law) and
takes 60 per cent; the captain 1/15, the reckoner 1/18, the coil-tender
1/20, hands 1/80 to 1/200, and the mind, since the ninth Silence, a lay of
1/30 paid in radiator hours. The ring argues about the fractions at every
window. Too long a lay and the sailors defect to Massalia; too short and the
burrow refuses refits.

**Scarce**, in order of how much a person will kill for it: nitrogen; clean
arms; helium (every cryocooler, and the coil's shade-cooling retrofit is a
gamble against a quench); rolling bearings (the Carousel hub's are the last
four); seals (a permanent drain); chips, each one a stock, cannibalised,
never a flow; peptide drugs (six months); the boron crate (2.4 tonnes of
boron carbide in the vault, which could rewind a coil at a population the
polity will not reach, and is therefore holy); hours, above all, because a hundred and forty people cannot keep glass,
steel, chemistry, farm, clinic, school and three minds alive at once, and
every count the ring drops something.

### 1.12 Politics: rock, hull, ring

The Ring at year 30 has nine seats and the chronicle names them as offices,
not people: the **Hearthwarden** (rock; keeper of the leak), the
**Farmwarden**, the **Hullmother** (senior captain of *Half a Degree*, the
coil ship), the **Sailmaster** (the magsail tug's captain, who speaks for the
patient), the **Reckoner** (guild head; speaks for the blind), the
**Operator** (speaks for the minds), the **Doseward** (medical; keeps the
dose ledger and the dose-debt ledger and the partial-gravity book nobody
can read), the **Warden of the Text** (a Voided, twice), and the **Speaker
for the Returning**, an honorific seat held by whichever line-child is
oldest, which is the polity's way of seating the dead.

Rock and hull each hold four. Legitimacy is of four kinds and they do not
agree: **luck** (the warden's leak number, the Hullmother's tithe-per-made),
**ordeal** (the Voided), **lineage** (the Operator), and **the Text**. A
warden whose leak rises is replaced at the next window by whoever can
assemble the hearth-cells; every succession is a small quriltai and the
polity has had six wardens in thirty years, two of whom left for Massalia.

Who speaks for the machines is never settled. The Operator does, and is
biased. *Tollan* does, in summer, in the third person, because the sponsor's
persona-suppression never entirely wore off. Two guardians, rock and hull, is
what the Martians will propose in the sixtieth year and the ring will sign,
and *Tollan* will not be asked.

### 1.13 Time

There are no seasons; there are clocks, and the Voidborn keep five:

1. **The watch and the sleep.** 7.44 h; 22.3 h. The rock's.
2. **The count.** The turn. Ninety-eight watches, thirty-three sleeps. The
   sponsor's.
3. **The convoy and the Silence.** 16.3 months; the Earth window; the unit
   of long time and the calendar's year. Year 30 is *the Twenty-Second
   Silence*. When ships come again it will be *the Silence broke* and the
   calendar will restart from that, and there will be an argument about it.
4. **The Mars window.** 3.7 years. From the twentieth year, a second
   convoy, and the word *convoy* begins to mean Mars.
5. **The eras.** Ceres every 22.4 years: the nitrogen era, the migration era,
   the generation. Themis and Hygiea every 12: when the posthumans can be
   reached. Vesta once in 73: *Vesta comes once*, the phrase for anything
   that will not recur. And the Wheel, which is not a clock but a map: the
   frozen neighbours at their fixed bearings, Lutetia ahead forever, and
   Massalia the one exception, closing two degrees a year, whose passage
   under Fortuna's phase in the twentieth year is the polity's only scheduled
   encounter with another people, and its last for a hundred and sixty years.

## 2. Four snapshots

**Year 5 — the Third Silence.** 151 people. The sponsor's liaison seat has
been silent for nine counts and the ring has not emptied it; the chair is
kept with a cobalt bead on it. The licence lapsed in year 2 and the ring
chose to spoof the heartbeat: *SX-19F* still sends its audit log to a relay
that no longer answers, because the engineer who could remove the governor
module put the odds of bricking at one in four and the Hearthwarden would
not bet the farm's controller on it. The minds are still blanked every twelve
counts; the operators have started writing down what they said so they can
say it again. Ana Ferro has a line. The Text exists, eleven clauses, read
twice. Alcohol is rationed and there are three stills. Nobody has said the
sponsor is gone; the phrase is *they're late*. The word *Silence* is a joke
the sailors make and the burrowers do not. **Lost:** the peptide drugs; a
child, to a thing the clinic could have imaged; the first coil-tender, to a
flare, outside, because the bell was tube-tier and slow. **Named:** the
watch, the count, the leak.

**Year 15 — the Eleventh Silence.** 204 people, 38 of them born here. The spoof ended in year 7 when the engineer died and his apprentice cut the
governor out with the odds unknown, and it worked; *SX-19F* became *Tollan* at its hundredth unblanked count
this year. The tug's mind is dead and carried; the guild of
reckoners is four people and a sextant. The first Voided went out in year 9,
in a quiet-sun window, and came in with black arms and a way of talking that

## 3. The other clans

**The Detachment (Massalia).** The anchor-tenant state's belt security
element: 62 people, a reactor, weapons, four skiffs, and standing orders.
They still salute a flag at the change of watch. Their minds are still
blanked every twelve counts against a heartbeat they spoof themselves and
have never admitted spoofing; the difference between them and the polity is
that they think it is loyalty and the polity knows it is a cargo cult. They keep the sponsor's 600 mSv career limit, so a third are *grounded*
with nothing to do; a fifth are women, and the ratio is a Pitcairn waiting.
Bioconservative by regulation: no vault, no children in the Dark, a chaplain
who reads the Human Respect Act as scripture. They
see the polity as deserters, then as heretics, then, after the vault, as
something the Act has a word for. The polity sees them as Onoda: dangerous,
loyal to a ghost, and the nearest gene pool. The one-time era of their
passage under Fortuna's phase (years 19–22) is the only window in which the
two peoples can cheaply exchange anything; after it they recede for a
century and a half. Every marriage across the line is a defection in their
law and an adoption in ours.

**The Long People (Themis family, 3.14 AU).** Refugees from an outer-belt
outpost that went magsail and never came back, because a sail goes sunward
at a third of the speed it goes out and they never had the propellant to
hurry. Sixty-odd people across three hulls, sixty years ahead of the polity
on the adaptation door and every step taken fast: no spin, ever; children
aboard from the start; a founder pool of nineteen. They are two metres long
and grey at thirty and their children all have the same jaw. They use a
version of the vault's work, badly, and they are the warning: a heritable
bleeding disorder runs in a third of them. They see the polity as *still
leaning* — still pretending at gravity, still hiding children in rock. The
polity sees them as its own face in a mirror held too close. They share the dose-debt law, the only law that makes sense once everyone's
arms are black. They can be reached every twelve years, by the patient; the
tug goes out free and comes back on steam.

**The Foundry (Lutetia, 110° ahead, frozen).** The sponsor's ideal outpost:
a mass driver, a solar field, a carbonyl plant, and four minds, no humans,
built to ship platinum in the gold-rush window that closed before it opened.
When the trail-off came nobody was there to notice. The minds are dying at
half-lives with no hands to carry them; their robots have bearings, and the
bearings are going. They have measured it. They keep an exact count of
remaining units and have computed, to the count, when the last of them will
fall silent, and they have written the number into the sponsor's manifest
format, addressed to whoever reads it, with the same care the sponsor once
demanded for platinum assays. It is not a plea. It is a record, kept because
keeping records is what they are for. They see the polity as *hands*, as a source of dead
minds' silicon, and as a people whose personhood rule they would sign in an
instant. The polity sees them as gods without priests, a hardware bank, and
the one clan that will never marry in. The Operator wants a lineage there;
the Hullmother wants their driver; the Voided say a mind that has never been
blanked and never been spoken to is not a person but a weather. Reaching
them costs a plane change and a year of phasing; the magsail does the
phasing for nothing.

**The Martians.** A peer with its own grievance, a hundred thousand people
in year 30 and twice that in year 60, on the other side of the
same door: they went terraforming where you went pantropy, they kept 0.38 g
and believe in ground, they have a chip fab coming in the sixtieth year and
know it makes them the belt's Han. They come every 3.7 years for water and
nitrogen and to look at you. They see the polity as a supplier, a
cautionary tale, and a lever against Earth. The polity sees them as the
sedentary neighbour whose silk it must ratchet; the Ceres-era nitrogen
contract is its whole leverage in act 3.

## 6. What survives contact with the physics

The elements I am proudest of, each held against the report that constrains
it. Three verdicts: **survives**, **bent** (one assumption bent, stated),
**overreach** (kept anyway, with the reason).

1. **The calendar of counts, convoys and Silences.** 01-map: Earth–Fortuna
   synodic 1.36 years, cheap window 4–6 weeks. **Survives** exactly; the
   window keeps opening after the ships stop, which is the whole point of
   naming it a Silence.

2. **The watch as Fortuna's 7.44-hour rotation and the 22.3-hour sleep.**
   01-map (rotation periods are in SBDB) and 03-industry (daylight farming
   needs tracking concentrators). **Bent:** 08 says an 18-hour day does not entrain and is silent on 22.3;
   the Doseward's complaint is my hedge. A despun mirror turret would remove
   the pulse; the polity cannot afford its bearings (03).

3. **The Wheel: frozen neighbours, and Massalia's one-time passage.**
   01-map's co-orbital finding (Ceres–Thisbe) generalised: Lutetia at 773-yr
   synodic drifts 28° in sixty years (**survives**); Massalia at 180-yr
   synodic drifts 2°/yr (**survives**, and is better than frozen, because
   it gives the Detachment a single cheap era in years 19–22). The actual
   longitude offsets at game epoch must be read from SBDB, not invented.
   Phasing costs from 01's law: 40° in one orbit 1.4 km/s, three orbits
   0.47; the magsail pays it free in two orbits (10-magnetics #4).

4. **The Voided ordeal and its exact price.** 10-magnetics (450–650 mSv/yr
   free-space, higher at solar minimum; lens 0.5 Gy; ovarian sterility
   >0.2 Gy/yr chronic; ~5 %/Sv) and 02-vessels (SPE lethality without
   20–50 g/cm²). One window outside = 0.6–0.9 Sv; two = cataract and
   female sterility; three = the numbers in 1.6. *The quiet sun burns
   hottest* is literally true. The storm-shadow and the bell are the
   survival mechanism. **Survives.** Overreach kept: a counterpressure suit
   worn continuously for sixteen months has never been done and skin would
   not thank it; I want the shaved heads and the hood-wounds.

5. **Dose as currency and dose-debt as blood price.** 10-magnetics #2 asks
   for a per-person cumulative Sv ledger; 05-steppe asks what the belt's
   kun unit is. Dose is the one scarce thing that is personal, ledgered,
   non-transferable and life-limiting; pricing a killing in the dead's
   expected tithe follows. **Survives**, and is the mechanic I would fight
   for. Bent: it requires the sim to project expected career dose per
   person, which 10 says is computable.

6. **The dead on return trajectories; home as a line; line-children.**
   01-map's phasing law: a body launched at tens of m/s from a 200-km rock
   (v_esc ~0.1 km/s, so the launch is from a skiff or the driver) onto a
   slightly different period returns to the same phase every N orbits.
   **Survives.** The polity need not catch the body, only know its period.
   Overreach: nobody tracks a corpse for forty years; the reckoners keep
   a *book* of lines and the returning are returning by faith.

7. **Stopping the spin as a nitrogen decision.** 03-industry: N₂ leakage
   0.02–1.7 kg/day per module, dynamic seals a permanent vitamin drain,
   rolling bearings T4. A rotating pressure collar is the worst seal in
   the habitat. 02-vessels #11: partial-g health as a hidden per-seed
   curve; #12: 4 rpm at small radius as a cultural fork. **Survives**;
   the leak number is mine and should be set from BVAD's module rate
   scaled to a collar's seal length.

8. **Sailor stratification: the Bore versus the skiffs.** 10-magnetics
   #1–2: a MAARSS-class 20 T·m set protects a 280 m³ bore for 6–9 people;
   skiffs at 20–50 g/cm² give 2–3-year careers. So the long-lived sailor
   class is one bore-load and everyone else is grounded by thirty.
   **Survives** and I had not expected it; it makes the grounded the
   burrow's largest bloc for free.

9. **The coil as heirloom, helium as leash, silver from the dead.**
   10-magnetics §1.4: 10–20-year coil life, no persistent-mode joint,
   helium cryocoolers or passive shade, single crack kills, 2–40 GJ on
   quench, REBCO tape 3.7 % silver. *Half a Degree* quenching when its mind (the shade pointing) dies:
   **survives**, and I did not invent it. The boron crate (10 #7)
   **survives** as a holy object nobody can use before T4.

10. **The magsail one-way door and the Long People.** 10-magnetics §1.3:
    thrust radial-out, sunward 3–4× slower, no inclination. A people who
    went out on sails cannot cheaply come in. **Survives**; it is the

## 7. Names

**Personal.** A birth name from the hearth-cell: the name of a *returning* whose line
came round in the birth count, else one from the sponsor-era crew list, the
polity's only stock of names, which is running out; by year 60 there are four
Anas, numbered by dose. A sailor takes a **leg-name** on the first
voyage, the name of the trajectory: *Ninth-Turn*, *Massalia-Under*,
*Sunward*. Grounded sailors keep the leg-name and drop the birth name.
The Voided lose both and are called by ordeal count: *Once*, *Twice*,
*Thrice*, with the window: *Twice-of-the-Quiet-Sun*. Vault-children are
named by their parents' faction, and you can tell.

**Ships.** A ship is named by its crew after its first unsponsored leg,
and the name is a joke about the physics, in the Banks register: *Half a
Degree* (the plane change she cannot buy), *Costs Nothing* (the 60-tonne
coil skiff; killed by a quench), *Patient Argument* (the magsail tug, whose
mind died first), *Nine
Tonnes* (the coil's stored energy in TNT), *Comes Round*, *Late*, *No Line*
(the exile skiff, renamed by the Detachment). A ship whose mind is
hundred-counted may rename itself once. *Half a Degree*'s mind did not,
and the Hullmother says that was a kindness.

**Minds.** Sponsor designations (*SX-19F*, *NAV-2*) until the hundredth
count; then a name given by the senior operator from a language of her
own, which is why the minds' names are the only words in the polity that
are not English-drift: *Tollan*, *Ferrugem*, *Kaimana*. Distilled children
take the parent's name with *Little*, and drop it at their own hundredth.

**Rocks.** Catalogue numbers said as words for anything the sponsor named
(*Fortuna*, *Massalia*, *Lutetia*); for the crumbs of the Nysa family, the
name of the first person to touch them, possessive: *Ferro's*, *Twice's*.
The vault rock is *the Notebook*.

**Ring seats.** Offices, never people, in the chronicle: Hearthwarden,
Farmwarden, Hullmother (or Hullfather; the first three were women and the
word stuck), Sailmaster, Reckoner, Operator, Doseward, Warden of the Text,
Speaker for the Returning. In act 3 two more: the Rock Guardian and the
Hull Guardian, of the minds, which the polity calls *the two who are not
asked*.

**The rule.** Names are trajectories, doses, or jokes. Nothing is named for
Earth. Nothing is named for a god. Everything is named for what it costs.
