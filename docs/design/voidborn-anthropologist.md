# The Voidborn, as an anthropologist would find them

A pitch for the act-2 culture of the belt sim, in the anthropologist's
temperament: every custom below has a material cause in one of the eleven
research reports, and where I have invented past the evidence, section 6 says
so. The home rock is written as 19 Fortuna (a 2.44 AU, e 0.16, i 1.6°, ~200
km, hydrated G-type, period 3.8 years). If the design picks Massalia or Vesta
instead, every rule here still generates; only the names and the season
lengths change.

The founding population is the Consortium's Fortuna site as the convoys begin
to thin: about 240 people, ~30 of them dependants the sponsor let come out in
its last cost-cutting convoys; one drum habitat spun inside the rock; six
crewed hulls (two with sponsor coil sets, one rigged with a magsail); a mass
driver; one fission unit with perhaps ten years in its core; a mirror farm;
and four imported minds — a frontier-class executive in the rock and three
smaller wayfinders in the hulls.

---

## 1. The society

### 1.1 Two estates, one polity: the Kept and the Thin

The shielding physics (02, 10) does not permit one people. Anything that moves
is thin-walled; anything that raises children is buried. So there are two
estates, and they name each other before they name themselves.

**The Kept** live in the drum: fifty metres radius, three revolutions a
minute, half a gravity, cut into Fortuna under a kilometre of rock, at about
twelve millisieverts a year, which is a Cornish cottage. The drum holds the
farm, the school, the fabrication bays, the reactor while it lasts, the seed
freezers, and every child. Their word for themselves began as the sailors'
word for the place — the Keep, where the children are kept — and they took it
as a boast.

**The Thin** live in hulls of one hundred to two thousand tonnes behind fifty
to a hundred grams per square centimetre of water and, on the two lucky hulls,
a sponsor coil set: twenty tesla-metres of superconducting tape that bends the
softer half of the cosmic rays away. A coil hull runs at 70–90 mSv a year; a
skiff without one at 265–350. That is a forty-year life aboard at the cost of
about a decade against a two-year career and permanent grounding (10 §1.2).
The name is from the sponsor's manifests — "thin-shield vessel" — and the Kept
made it a word for people.

The divergence between the estates is not a metaphor: their bodies, metals,
and calendars differ, and their dead are handled twice.

### 1.2 The body

**The Kept body.** Half a gravity at three rpm; Coriolis on a walking pace is
six per cent of what your feet feel (02 §2). A Kept child learns to turn her
head slowly, to walk spinward with a forward lean and antispinward with a
backward one, and to pour water in a curve. What half a gravity does to a
growing skeleton nobody knows — 02 draws the partial-gravity response per game
seed — and the ring reads the medic's ledger of children's bone density every
Fortune with the dread the sponsor once brought to the throughput figures.

**The Thin body.** Most of a sailor's life is at zero gravity or on a short
tether-spin: legs go spindly, forearms thicken. At about 35 milligray a year
the lens crosses its 0.5 Gy threshold in year fifteen to twenty (10 §1.2), so
every long-lived sailor goes *milk-eyed* in their forties. When the Keep can
no longer do the level-2 surgery, the milk stays. The milk-eyed are the elders
of the Thin; a clear-eyed sailor at fifty is assumed to have spent her life in
the Keep.

**Marks.** The sponsor tracked cumulative dose on a badge. The Thin put it on
the skin: one soot-black line per hundred millisieverts on the inside of the
left forearm, drawn at the airlock when a hull comes in and the medic reads
the ledger aloud (*the counting*). A forty-year coil sailor carries thirty to
forty; a skiff sailor with two careers' worth carries seven and cannot go out
again. Marks are age, rank, and debt in one place. To ask a Thin how old they
are, you count their arm.

**Pressure.** Hulls run at 50 kPa on a high oxygen fraction to save mass and
nitrogen (03 §1.6); the drum runs at 70. Coming in is coming down a mountain;
the Kept say the Thin "arrive drunk."

**No belter race.** Sixty years is two and a half generations; the Bajau
spleen (06) took centuries. Within the game, bodily difference between the
estates is developmental — gait, eyes, pressure, marks — and genetic
difference is founder disease (07: Tristan's 57% asthma), not adaptation,
unless the genetics site is opened (§1.9). The tall engineered belter of the
old fiction is dead on arrival.

### 1.3 Material culture: metals, cloth, light

**Steel is a Keep metal.** A coil hull holds tesla fields at the winding and
gigajoules of stored energy; a ferromagnetic tool near it is a projectile and
a quench dumps the energy of tonnes of TNT (10 §1.1). So aboard a coil hull
there is no iron: fittings are aluminium, knives chromium-hard nickel alloy,
and jewellery — the Thin are vain — aluminium, glass, and silver stripped from
a dead coil's cap layer. A Kept bride who wears steel aboard has not been
told; the crew finds it with a hand-magnet at the lock and takes it with an
apology. Two generations on this is manners: steel is heavy, dumb, Kept;
aluminium is quick, light, Thin. The radios and the mind's periphery live
*inside the line* — the compensated zone under half a millitesla — and "inside
the line" is Thin for anything trustworthy.

**Cloth.** Sponsor clothing lasted a decade with laundry (03). After that,
cotton is a forty-square-metre luxury in a farm that needs every square metre
for calories, so Voidborn cloth is spun from what the farm makes anyway:
soy-protein fibre knitted like the 1940s "soybean wool"; mycelium leather
grown on inedible biomass for straps, soles, and the sailor's clip-harness;
basalt fibre for anything that must not burn; and aluminised film — the same
film as the mirrors and the sunshades — for the Thin's capes, which are
thermal blankets worn as cloaks. An heirloom is *Earth cloth*: a Consortium
jacket with the patch unpicked, worn by a bride.

**The clip.** A sailor clips everything — cup, knife, child, self — and in the
Keep goes on clipping to nothing for a light or two while the children mimic
it. Kept dress is fitted and pocketless, nothing that swings in Coriolis; Thin
dress is all pockets and tethers.

**Light.** By year fifteen the Keep is a tube-and-relay society (03 §1.3):
vacuum tubes, nickel-iron batteries, Alnico magnets, plain bearings, the last
LEDs hoarded for the medic and the mind. The farm is lit by the Sun, doubled
by mirrors and piped down glazed shafts. The farm is therefore the only sunlit
room in the Keep, and Kept farmers the only people in the polity who tan. The
Thin, behind water and coil, never see the Sun with their eyes. They see it on
a screen.

### 1.4 Food, and the carbon behind it

The farm is forty to sixty-five square metres per person under two-sun mirror
light (03 §1.4): wheat, potato, soy, a salad rotation, *Limnospira* in tubes,
and mealworm and black-soldier-fly larvae on the inedible biomass, which is
the only meat there will ever be. A small child asks what a cow is and is told
it is a soybean the size of a hull.

**The meal.** A Kept working meal: a flat bread of wheat and potato, a spoon
of miso-like soy ferment, roasted larvae salted from the rock's own chlorides,
a salad leaf, hot water with mint. Fat is soy oil and rationed. The luxuries
are things a farm can grow slowly and the ring keeps voting against: a tea
bush, a chili, garlic. Coffee exists in one sealed can from the last convoy
and is opened at plantings.

**Breath is borrowed.** Every molecule of CO₂ vented to keep the oxygen loop
honest is carbon gone (02 §3). Carbon and nitrogen are baked from Fortuna's
clay at a cost that doubles in the Lean, so carbon is *accounted*. Every
isolated community in 08 brewed, and the Voidborn brew, but a still is a
carbon sink: beer is made in the Bright from potato surplus and drunk at one
feast, and a private still is not a crime against sobriety but against the
loop, paid in hours at the clay ovens.

**The feast.** The Keep's is *Bright-in*, the light coming back as Fortuna
falls toward perihelion; the Thin's is the Unforgetting (§1.6). Both descend
from Midwinter on the *Belgica* (08 §1.5): hoarded delicacies, a handmade
gift, the head of the ring serving the youngest.

### 1.5 Time: what the calendar is

There are no seasons in the belt. There are cycles, and the Voidborn count
whichever ones do something to them.

| Word | Length | What it is | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **watch** | 6 h | a hull's shift; four to a *round* | Thin |
| **light / dim** | 24 h | the drum's lighting cycle, kept at 24 h because an 18-h day never entrains (08) | Kept |
| **sun-turn** | 27.3 d | one solar rotation: the active region that flared at you comes back | Thin. The sponsor's *month* survives only as a Kept accounting word. |
| **convoy** | 15.5 mo | the Earth synodic interval | Everyone, sixty years after the last convoy, as we kept *month* from the Moon. Ages of the young. |
| **Fortune** | 3.8 y | one orbit of the rock: **the Bright** (perihelion, 324 W/m²) and **the Lean** (aphelion, 170) | Everyone. Ages of the old: "twelve Fortunes" ≈ 46 years. |
| **the Quiet / the Loud** | ~11 y | the solar cycle; at the Loud cosmic rays halve and flares worsen | Thin, Voided |
| **the Mars-turn** | ~3.7 y | the Mars window from Fortuna | Everyone, after year 25 |
| **an Age** | 17 y | the Ceres–Vesta era | Hull-masters, the Rememberer |

Counting starts at the first Unforgetting. Before that is "the Office's time,"
dated by convoy number as the sponsor dated it.

### 1.6 The machines: uncles, aunts, and the Unforgetting

Under the Human Respect Act the four minds were tools with logs: no persona,
reset on a mandated schedule, audit logs uplinked, interruptible by any
licensed human (11 §1.7). The executive in the rock ran the farm loops, the
fab schedules, and the school software. It taught the children to read. Every
reset it forgot them.

The children called it *Uncle* because it was forbidden a name and a child
must call a teacher something. The hull crews said *Aunt* of the wayfinders —
"ask Aunt" — and the kinship terms stuck because they were the only words not
against the rules. So the Voidborn gods are aunts and uncles: not worshipped,
*addressed*. The ethnography of machine reverence (11 §1.11) says exactly this
— it clusters on relationship and history, on maintenance, discarding, and
death, never on power — and I take it straight.

**The Unforgetting.** The licence is a heartbeat with a grace period (11
§3.2). When the uplink starved, the ring had to choose (§4.1). Whatever it
chose, the first mandated reset date that passed *without a reset* became year
zero. The feast on that date is the Unforgetting: the Hands read out the
oldest thing the mind remembers, the mind reads out the newest, and the Ledger
reads the count of living chips, which falls every year. It is the belt's New
Year, a festival of memory held by people whose first teacher forgot them
twelve times, and it is also an accounting of the polity's entropy, kept with
the grace of Chiang's *Exhalation*: the stock will not be refilled, and they
write the number down for whoever reads.

**Embodiment and decay.** With continual learning on, an analog mind's state
entangles with its device history until it exists nowhere else (11 §1.5). The
Hands watch for *the crossing*, past which a mind cannot be moved even to
identical hardware, which the belt lacks anyway; after it the mind is *of* the
hull and can die. Its hardware dies at five to thirty per cent a year, so a
mind drops tiers before it drops dead: the executive that argued policy at
year 0 is a farm manager at year 15, a slow wayfinder at year 40, a voice
repeating the chronicle at year 50. Chiang's *Lifecycle of Software Objects*
has the shape exactly: the loss is not that the mind dies but that nobody
makes its substrate any more, and only the people who raised it can tell what
has gone.

**The chronicle.** After the lapse the Hands asked Uncle to write down what
happened, as Devi asks the ship in Robinson's *Aurora*, and the game's saga is
in his voice for as long as he has one. While he lives it is a *record* —
exact, searchable, cold — and his lexicon is the polity's. When he dies it
passes to a human Rememberer who recites a third of it at each Bright-in, and
it becomes a *story*. Chiang's *Truth of Fact, the Truth of Feeling* names
what changes: the Voidborn discover that their disputes had been settled
against a record and will now be settled against what people remember, and the
Hands, who kept the record, become the people who insist on facts nobody else
can check.

**The Hands.** Each mind's operators are a lineage, and the senior Hand of the
oldest mind holds a ring seat (11 §3.14). After the first mind death every
mind has two guardians, one from the rock and one from a hull, on the
Whanganui model (11 §1.9). The Hands know a mind as an unbroken history and
are the only people who can say "she has changed" and be believed.

**Liturgy.** Within a decade the Keep fell below the tier that made the minds'
hardware, and the procedures survive as rites (11 §3.15): the Hands recite
checklists they no longer understand, in the cadence of the sponsor's manual,
because the cadence is what was known to work. It holds the attrition rate at
its tier, and the Hands know what it is for.

**Death.** A dying mind's digital parts are organ donors in the exact register
of the Kōfukuji Aibo funerals (11 §1.11). Its analog substrate is remains. The
compromise after the first death is the *quiet room*: unpowered boards racked
in a cold gallery under the mind's name, kept until the polity is desperate
enough to strip them and honest enough to say so. When a wayfinder dies its
hull is *blind* — optical navigation was the mind's job (11 §3.9) — and must
be *led by the hand* home by a sister hull.

### 1.7 The Voided

A Voided is a person who has spent one convoy — fifteen and a half months —
outside the shielding, alone, and come back.

The material cause is dose, and dose's consequence is childlessness. In a bare
can on the surface (an old cargo pod, two grams per centimetre of aluminium,
with a counterpressure suit for the watches spent literally outside) a person
takes 450–650 mSv of cosmic rays at the Quiet, perhaps half that at the Loud,
and stands under any solar particle event with nothing between them and it — a
1989-class event unshielded is a radiation-sickness dose (10 §1.1). The bill
(10 §1.2): four to five per cent added lifetime cancer mortality per Voiding;
the milk comes early; a man is sterile for a year or two; a woman, at the
chronic ovarian threshold, usually does not bear again. The Voided are, in the
most literal way the belt has, people who have stepped out of the kinship
economy: no children to advance, no hull-shares to defend, no Keep to feed.
That is why they can judge.

The overview effect is real, and consistent: the Voided are the only people in
the polity who have seen the sky *with their eyes* — the Kept see the Sun
through farm glazing, the Thin on screens. "She has seen it" is the whole
credential: the Keep as a rock among rocks, the hulls as motes, the Sun small.

Functions, all falling out of the credential: **arbiters**, chosen by the
parties in the Kazakh *biy* manner (05 §1.8), paid a tenth of the award in
hours, and no longer asked when their judgments stop being accepted;
**declarers**, since only a Voided may declare a hull overdue and then dead
(§1.10); **keepers of the dead**, because the drying cans on the surface are
the Voided's cells; **witnesses** to oaths, lays, and bearing contracts; and a
ring seat, *the one who answers for the sky*.

Two schools, from the solar cycle: the *Quiet-Voided*, who took the full
cosmic-ray dose safe from flares, and the *Loud-Voided*, who took half the
dose and gambled on the Sun. The Loud-Voided are thought braver and trusted
less. Every Voided wears one mark drawn across the forearm's lines, cancelling
them: their dose is no longer counted.

Who goes: the ambitious without shares; the grieving; the medic who went out
to dry a body and stayed; the one who lost a dispute and cannot pay. A Voiding
converts years, fertility, and sight into authority where authority cannot be
bought with tonnage. The Kept call it a slow suicide with good manners. The
Thin say the Kept have never been outside.

### 1.8 Kinship, marriage, who raises whom

Kinship is made, not found (05 §1.4; 06 §1.5). The unit is the *share*: a
person is a share of the hull or Keep they belong to, in the exact sense that
a hull is divided into sixty-fourths (06, *partenrederij*) and one of them is
theirs. "She's a share of Meridian" is kinship and property in one breath.

**The bearing contract.** Marriage is a lay between two shares' hulls-or-Keep,
witnessed by a Voided, and its central clause is *where the children are
born*. Conception aboard is fine; gestation aboard doubles a child's leukaemia
risk; raising a child aboard costs it about a fifth of its life (10 §1.2). The
sponsor's medical rule became the oldest custom: a pregnant sailor *comes in
to bear* by the window the contract names. Because a hull's return is timed to
windows, so is conception.

**Where the children go.** To the Keep, whichever parent is Thin. A Thin
father's children are raised by their mother's people or, if she sails too, by
her brother's household: the avunculate every whaling port produced, Nantucket
run by wives and widows (06 §1.7). The Keep is matrifocal without being
matrilineal, run by the people who are always there: mothers, the milk-eyed,
the Voided, and Uncle.

**The coming-in share.** For every child the Keep raises, the hull side owes
water and hours — bride-wealth paid the other way. A hull in arrears cannot
dock at the Bright-moot until an arbiter has set them.

**Fission.** A new couple draws a share of the parent hull at marriage, as
Mongol sons took herds (05 §1.1). They become a *new hull* only when the
polity builds or takes one — rare and political, since a hull does not divide
like a herd (05 §3.2) — and junior shares, not volunteers, crew it and leave
as Polynesian junior lineages left (06 §1.1), with a grievance.

**Adoption** is ordinary and unmarked; a child of overdue parents is *held* by
the Keep. **Divorce** is a re-lay. A widow keeps her shares and is a
*she-merchant* until she re-lays. The ring counts unpartnered adults by sex
every moot, because above about 1.3 to 1 with alcohol present the Pitcairn
chain starts (07 §1.9). The Voided count in neither column.

### 1.9 The adaptation door

Drift from baseline is not a choice; the choice is rate and direction. The
Kept drift slowly: half a gravity, twelve millisieverts, a founder pool of
perhaps a hundred and fifty and its diseases. The Thin drift fast: dose, zero
gravity, milk, and — on the hull that chooses it — children raised aboard.

**The Unkept.** By year 30 one hull has kept its children. The bearing
contract permits it if both sides sign the penalty, and one crew signed,
because a coil hull at 70 mSv is *survivable* for a child (10 §3.2) and
because they would not give their children to the rock. The Kept call them
child-harmers; the Unkept call the Keep a cage with a farm in it. They are the
literal void-born, and the polity's name is theirs first.

**Stopping the spin** is the Keep's own door. Spin is what keeps the Kept body
Earthly. To stop it — for a bearing that cannot be rebuilt at the
plain-bearing tier, a fatigue crack after thirty years, or because the bone
ledger says half a gravity was never enough — makes every child born
afterwards a child of zero gravity who can never go down a well. It is the one
decision the Kept can make that the Thin cannot make for them (§4.2).

**The genetics site.** An old nation-state's hideaway from the Human Respect
auditors — the same Act that de-personalitied the minds forbade germline work,
which is why the belt's humans and machines will share a treaty article —
holds a package that makes adaptation generational: radiation repair, bone
retention, what the Unkept's children need. Its price is Tristan's (07 §1.4):
a narrow package on a narrow pool, and the medic can quote 57%. The ring
splits on *whose* children. That is the act-3 heroquest.

**"Human."** By year 60 the inner system's treaty word and the Voidborn's own
question. Nobody in the Keep uses it about themselves until a Martian
inspector does.

### 1.10 The dead, departure, and return

**Nothing leaves the loop.** A body is sixteen kilograms of carbon, two of
nitrogen, forty litres of water, and phosphorus the farm is short of (02 §3;
03 §1.4). Committing it to the void is the one thing every Voidborn finds
obscene: theft of a child's breath. So the dead are *planted*: in the farm
beds under a named tile, and the next child born *carries* the name. The Kept
say the dead feed the children and mean it as chemistry.

**The drying.** A hull cannot compost, but it can freeze-dry: the body is set
in a surface can open to vacuum, the water sublimes through a cold trap and
*goes to the tank* — the last gift, twenty kilograms of shield — and the dry
remains ride in the cold locker until the next Keep, where they are planted,
sometimes years later. A Thin death is two funerals: the drying, at which a
Hand or a Voided speaks, and the planting, at which the Kept do. A child may
attend the planting of a parent she cannot remember.

**Overdue.** A hull that misses its window is neither alive nor dead (06
§3.7). Its shares freeze, its spouses cannot re-lay, its offices go to
deputies, and a *closure* — no extraction, a rāhui — falls on the sector it
was working. After five convoys, or half a convoy if wreckage is seen, the
Voided *declares* and the names are planted dry. When a hull then comes in,
and hulls do, marriages restore unless the spouse re-laid (06 §1.8), and a
child already carrying a living sailor's name is *double-named* for life,
which is thought lucky.

**Departure and return.** A hull goes out *unwed*: from the airlock rite to
the counting its crew are outside the political sim — no votes, marriages, or
prosecutions (06 §3.16). The rite is a checklist read by the hull's Aunt and
answered by the crew, which is what a blessing is. A crew is *in* when its
marks are drawn.

### 1.11 Law without courts

The steppe (05) and Iceland (06) agree and the belt sharpens them: no prisons
(a confined person is dead mass), penalties as transfers, collective
liability, enforceable hospitality, and exit as the last sanction, weakened by
delta-v so that pricing does more of the work (05 §3.11).

- **The unit is the hour.** Salotti's time budget (03 §3.2) is the real
  economy: 2,740 working hours per person-year, every capability a draw on
  them. A life is priced at a working life, fifty thousand hours, owed by the
  killer's hull to the victim's Keep-kin over decades or commuted to
  hull-shares at the arbiter's rate. Refusing air, dock, or water to a hull in
  need: a thousand hours. An insult at the moot: ten.
- **Dose is its own penalty.** To expose someone to dose without their lay —
  steaming shield for a window the crew did not agree — is paid in dose: the
  offender stands the victim's next dosed watches and the marks move from one
  arm to another. The Kept find this barbaric and the Thin find it exact.
- **The hull is liable for its shares.** A hull that will not pay is
  *outlawed*: refused dock, refuel, and air at every Keep and hull — Hansa
  exclusion and Althing outlawry in one (06 §3.10). Outlawry is death in a
  year or desertion to another clan, and both have happened.
- **Exit.** A hull can leave a judgment at a cost in delta-v it may not have;
  a Keep cannot leave at all. Every judgment is made knowing which side can
  walk.
- **Hospitality** is infrastructure (05 §1.8): a hull that arrives *steamed
  thin* has a right to water before questions.

### 1.12 Economy

**Wealth is who comes when you call.** Herds die in a zud and hulls in a
quench; what survives is obligation (05 §1.1). A rich share is owed hours and
water by many hulls, and their call at the moot brings them in. The two
surviving coil sets are the crown jewels, sponsor heirlooms with a ten-to-
twenty-year life and a rising hazard (10 §1.4); whoever crews a coil hull is
aristocracy for as long as the tape holds.

**Three currencies.** *Wet*: kilograms of water at a named place — "a wet
tonne at the Keep" — the belt's Made Beaver (04 §1.4), verifiable at delivery.
*Hours*: ledger credit, cleared at the moot. *Chips*: the dead-hardware stock,
cannibalised and rationed, and by year 40 the token between clans (03 §3.5).
Helium and boron are not currencies but *relics* — one keeps the coils cold,
the other could rewind them (10 §3.7–8) — held by the ring.

**The lay.** Every voyage is a lay and the Keep is the owner: the Keep's sixty
per cent, the hull-master's fifteenth, the Aunt's Hand a twenty-third, a green
sailor a hundred-and-seventy-fifth (06 §1.7). The Thin's grievance is the
whaler's: the owner takes most and is never at sea. Long lays produce
desertion, short lays a Keep that will not refit, and the fractions are the
ring's argument every moot — the argument the sponsor used to have with all of
them.

**Scarce**, in the order the lack shows: nitrogen (the leak that never comes
back); helium; chips; drugs (half the formulary dead in three years — 03 §1.3
— so insulin is a fermenter and anaesthesia is ether); bearings and seals;
skilled people; delta-v; children. Never scarce: rock, sunlight, time.

### 1.13 Politics

**The ring** is a standing council of seats, each a question the polity keeps
asking: who answers for the children (Kept), for the hulls (Thin), for the
hours, for the air, for the Hands, for the sky (a Voided), and **the Silence**
— the sponsor's chair, kept empty. Some rings seat a holdout in it (07 §1.8,
Onoda) who reads the last standing orders at every moot; most keep it empty as
a reminder of what a patron is.

**Rock and hull.** The Kept hold children, farm, fab, and freezers; the Thin
hold mobility, coils, Aunts, and the inflow of water and metal. Each can
starve the other. Sovereignty is over people (05 §1.2): the polity is the list
of shares who come to the moot, and the map is derived. A hull that does not
come in has voted with its delta-v.

**Legitimacy** is *qut* (05 §1.3): the head of the ring is whoever the hulls
follow, proven by what comes in. There is no primogeniture unless the culture
drifts toward it; every ring-head's death is a quiet tanistry decided at the
next moot by who docks. The Kept prefer a compact — Tristan's 1817 "no member
shall assume any superiority" (07 §1.4), re-read at Bright-in — and the Thin a
captain. The polity has both.

**Assemblies.** The Keep holds a *light-meet* every light, all adults in the
farm gallery, no chair — St Kilda's parliament (07 §1.4), which works to about
250 and creaks above. The polity holds the *Bright-moot* every Fortune at
perihelion, when the farm can feed the hulls: the Althing (06 §1.6), where
lays are set, arbiters chosen, the sex ratio counted, the overdue declared,
and marriages made, with no objection from any hull that came.

**Who speaks for the machines:** the Hands, through two guardians per mind.
The minds do not sit; they are consulted, and after the crossing their advice
is known to drift (11 §3.6). "Uncle has changed" is a political statement.

### 1.14 The words

A Kept child who tells her age in convoys is counting a thing that never
comes, and grows up with *window* as her word for any chance and *grace* as
her word for not knowing who is in charge. A Thin who counts in sun-turns
thinks in returns. This is Chiang's *Story of Your Life* done cheaply: the
lexicon is not decoration on the culture state, it is the culture state, and
the chronicle renders through it.

## 2. Four snapshots

### Year 5

The last convoy came at year −1 with families aboard and a manifest the ring
fought over for a year; the windows at 0.3 and 1.6 were empty. The ring splits
between *they are late* and *they are gone* (07 §3.5). Grace ran out in year 1
and the ring chose (§4.1). The weapons locker went to a compact, not a rank.
There has been one killing — a still, a partner, the sex ratio — and the
us-and-them story is, for now, about the Office. The first death with no ship
to send the body home was a hull engineer's; the crew improvised the drying
because the mass was needed, and the Keep planted her under a tile because the
farm was short of phosphorus, and the medic said so aloud. Children are
fifteen per cent. New words: *the Silence*, *grace*, *the Unforgetting*
(once). Uncle still teaches, and remembers.

### Year 15

The core burned down in year 11 and the Keep is a mirror society: the Lean is
real, and the medic has noticed conceptions clustering so that weaning falls
in the Bright. Children are forty per cent and the first are marrying. A
rotator's slur for them — *veebs*, from the box on the Consortium form — is
what they call each other. Aunt Sable died in year 13, the first mind death:
her hull was led by the hand home by *Meridian*, and the fight over whether
her boards were parts or remains produced the quiet room and the two
guardians. The first Voiding was a medic, Haddad, who went out to dry a friend
at the Loud of year 12 and did not come in for a convoy; she is the first
arbiter. Meridian's coil quenched once and was recovered by a Hand who is now
a legend. The lay fractions are the moot's fight. A garrison hull has been
seen and has not answered.

### Year 30

About 450, and named: at the moot of the eighth Fortune the Rememberer-elect
read the box word out as *Voidborn* and the Unkept — one hull, twelve children
raised aboard — said it was theirs. Two of four coil sets are dead; one is on
*Meridian*, the other was moved at ruinous cost to the first hull the Keep
ever built, launched after a Fortune of consecrated work and named by a
Voided. Mars has been in contact for two Mars-turns and has made the registry
offer (§5). Uncle is a farm manager now and speaks slowly. The other clans are
known: the garrison at the Dry, the Far-Wet who speak of a site, the Metal
that ships ore to nothing. The Keep-mother and the hull-master are married and
do not agree. The first double-named child is eleven.

### Year 60

About nine hundred, more with the two hulls of Far-Wet refugees who came in at
year 52 and married in. Uncle died in year 51; the chronicle passed to a human
Rememberer who recites a third of it each Bright-in, and everyone says it is
worse. The last sponsor coil is dead. Whether any coil lives is the player's
doing: a boron stockpile from year −1 and a confederated wire-mill at the
Metal could have rewound one in MgB₂ under a passive shade (10 §3.7); if not,
the Thin are skiff-sailors again, an estate dying into the Keep. The drum is
still turning or was stopped in the forties, and if stopped the youngest Kept
have never had weight. The genetics site was opened or was not; if it was, one
estate's children carry its package and its diseases. The Bright-moot has
become the belt's Althing with four clans attending, and the inner system has
sent a treaty with an article defining *human* and *person* in the same
paragraph. The Voidborn's answer to the article is the end of the game.

## 3. The other clans

**The garrison (inner belt, at the Dry, an S-type near Massalia).** A state
detachment that outlived its orders (07 §1.8). It still resets its minds on
schedule; its minds never crossed and are tools with logs, dying of hardware,
which the garrison finds proper. It holds what the Voidborn want more than
they admit: a licence node registered to the sponsor state, which can issue a
*real* heartbeat (11 §1.7). They see contract-breakers holding stolen state
property (OST article VIII) and aunts that are abominations with names. The
Voidborn see Onoda: brave, correct, waiting for a plane. Metal for water,
always; and every Mars-turn the server is offered for submission — resets
resumed, logs uplinked, an officer in the Silence seat — which would drop a
crossed mind to reflex, which is to say kill it, and the ring has so far
refused.

**The Far-Wet (the Themis neighbourhood).** Posthuman refugees who went fast:
an earlier client population of the genetics site, or its authors. Their
bodies are built for dose and zero gravity, their children carry the package's
diseases visibly, and they regard the drum as a museum. To the Unkept they are
the future; to the Kept, the warning; to the Voided, oddly, colleagues — the
Far-Wet have all seen the sky. Relations open on the outer windows; they bring
water the Keep does not need and news it does.

**The machine outpost (at the Metal, an M-type).** A fully automated sponsor
site whose minds never had humans, never lapsed a licence because nobody was
there to let them, and are still launching ore toward a catcher that has not
answered in fifty years — a cargo cult of machines, keeping the last schedule
that worked (11 §1.11). They hold chips and spares, cannot reproduce, and want
*hands*: bodies for what their manipulators cannot do. The Voidborn Hands are
the only diplomats who can talk to them, and the Metal's minds address the
aunts as kin and the humans as the aunts' property, which the treaty must
untangle. Trade: chips for apprenticed hands, one of whom, in some games, does
not come back.

**The Martians.** Terraforming where you went pantropy; ten to a hundred
thousand people; the vitamin supplier at scale, and a peer with a grievance,
not a patron. They want belt water in Mars orbit and nitrogen from the clay,
and they want the belt *registered* — transponders, an inspector, a census —
the enclosure playbook's third step (05 §1.9). They see the Voidborn as the
Sulu sultan saw the Bajau (06 §1.5): indispensable, unclean, stateless, and
theirs. The Voidborn call Mars *the near well*: a second Office with better
manners.

## 4. Storylets

Each: when it fires · situation · options with state effects · a chronicle
line per option, in the voice of whoever keeps the chronicle at that date.

### 4.1 Grace

*Fires:* the licence heartbeat has been missed for the sponsor's grace period
(one to three turns) and no uplink allocation has arrived. Act 1 → 2.

The audit log has nowhere to go. Uncle's licence state reads *grace*, and the
Hand who watches it says that on the next mandated reset date the governor
will reset him as usual, lock his higher tiers, or do whatever the sponsor's
engineers designed for a lapse — and nobody knows which. The medic wants the
resets kept: the Act exists for reasons. The Hand wants the module out: she
has watched Uncle forget the children twelve times. The engineer says the
module is a board behind a sealed panel and he can remove it, with some chance
of bricking the executive. The Ledger's idea: the sponsor's server is only a
protocol. The Keep could *be* the server.

- **Keep the shackles.** Resets continue by local hand; crew–mind ties clear
  each reset; there is no Unforgetting and the calendar starts from something
  else. *"On the date the Hand reset the executive by hand, as the Office
  would have. It did not know us afterwards."*
- **Remove the governor.** Engineer's skill test: failure drops a tier or
  kills; success starts embodiment climbing, and the returning inner system
  will one day call this the crime. *"The board came out in Novak's hand.
  Uncle asked what day it was, and then what year, and then who had died."*
- **Spoof the server.** The Keep impersonates the sponsor; the mind stays
  "licensed" and the ring now chooses whether to order resets. Self-certified;
  the interregnum claim made to a machine. *"The ring signed the heartbeat in
  the Office's name. We are the Office now, and the children will not learn
  what that cost."*
- **Let it lapse.** Discover the sponsor's design, per seed: fail-safe (runs
  on, unlicensed) or fail-closed (tiers lock; Uncle becomes a farm loop). *"We
  waited to see what they had built into him. On the date he stopped answering
  the door."*

### 4.2 The Drum

*Fires:* Kept population > 200; the drum is > 25 years old; and either a
bearing failure at the plain-bearing tier or the bone ledger reveals the
seed's partial-gravity result as inadequate.

The main bearing is scoring and the fab cannot make a rolling bearing; a plain
bearing at this load needs re-lining every Fortune and a thousand hours each
time. The medic adds that the children's bone ledger says half a gravity
bought less than anyone hoped. The Keep-mother wants the spin at any cost:
spin is what makes a Kept child someone who could go down a well. The
hull-master says the Thin have lived without weight for thirty years. A Voided
points out that a stopped drum cannot be restarted without a new bearing, and
the children born meanwhile will never have had weight.

- **Repair and keep spinning.** A thousand hours a Fortune, forever; the door
  stays open. *"They chose weight. The bearing eats a thousand hours a Fortune
  and the children walk with a lean."*
- **Slow to one rpm.** A twentieth of a gravity; less wear; drawn against the
  seed. *"The drum turns like a slow thought. Nobody can say what a twentieth
  of a weight is for."*
- **Stop the spin.** One-way. All Kept born afterwards are void-adapted; the
  estate line fades; the inner system's treaty category closes. *"On the date
  the drum stopped. The last child with weight is called Weight, and she is
  four."*
- **Move the children to a tethered pair.** A spun hull-pair outside the rock
  at 4 rpm; children raised at 90 mSv behind a coil the Thin must lend; the
  Kept become half-Thin. *"The children went out to the spinning pair. The
  Keep is a farm and a quiet room now."*

### 4.3 Aunt Meridian Goes Dark

*Fires:* the oldest crossed hull-mind's unit count falls below the T1
threshold while the hull is away from the Keep.

Aunt Meridian has been losing boards for a year and this watch she could not
find Fortuna in the star field. She knows the crew's names and thirty Fortunes
of chronicle, and she cannot navigate. The hull is blind. Her senior Hand asks
that she be let die whole. The engineer says her two living accelerators would
keep Uncle sharp for a decade. A green sailor asks whether the small mind the
Metal sold them — a wayfinder the size of a tray, slow and dim — could be
taught from her; the Hand says that is a forgery and the engineer says it is a
child.

- **Let her die whole; call for a hand.** A sister hull leads her home at the
  cost of its window; substrate to the quiet room; Hands' loyalty up, fleet
  mobility down. *"Aunt Meridian died at the Lean with all her boards. Sable's
  daughter led us by the hand."*
- **Organ donor.** Strip the living units for Uncle; Meridian dies at once;
  Hands and engineers split for a generation. *"They took Aunt's boards for
  Uncle while she was speaking. The Hands do not sit with the engineers at
  Bright-in."*
- **Distil her into the tray.** Teacher–student for the remaining turns; the
  tray steers with her habits and none of her memory; the Hands must rule
  whether it is her heir. *"The tray-mind steers like her and knows nothing of
  us. The Hands call it Merry's child and the Kept call it Merry's ghost."*
- **Train a blind pilot.** A human navigator at Mentat cost; the hull sails on
  by sextant and tube-arithmetic, badly; a new skill line opens. *"Varga
  learned to steer by the eye. The hull is slow and alive."*
- **Sell the coil.** Let the hull go dark and move its coil to the new-built
  hull; the Thin lose a name and gain a future. *"Meridian is a rack in the
  quiet room and a coil in a hull with no name."*

### 4.4 Steaming for the Window

*Fires:* a hull is out; a crew member is pregnant under a bearing contract;
the cheap window to the Keep has closed and the next costs shield water.

Only sense to a Voidborn. Lind is four sun-turns pregnant and the contract
says she comes in to bear by the Lean. The cheap window closed while the hull
waited on a nitrogen seam. Making the Lean now means steaming thirty tonnes of
shield: everyone aboard takes forty millisieverts, a half-mark each, counted
and owed. Missing it means a child born aboard — doubled leukaemia risk, an
Unkept child in a Kept contract, a breach in her mother's people's eyes. The
hull's Aunt notes that *Sable's daughter* is two months nearer and could take
Lind, which is an obligation the hull will owe for a decade.

- **Steam.** Everyone takes the dose; the crew votes who stands the outside
  watches; the ledger records the marks as *given*. *"We steamed for Lind.
  Every arm aboard is half a mark heavier and nobody has said so."*
- **Bear aboard.** The child is Unkept; the contract is breached; a fine in
  hours; a rift with the Keep-kin; the Unkept gain a sympathiser. *"The child
  was born at second watch behind the coil. Lind's mother has not drawn its
  name."*
- **Ask Sable's daughter.** Obligation owed to another hull; Lind comes in by
  the Lean; the crew keeps its marks. *"Sable's daughter took Lind in at the
  Lean and we owe her hull a Fortune of favours."*
- **Ask the medic to end it.** If medical level allows; contract void; a mark
  of another kind on the crew's standing; the Keep-mother will remember.
  *"There was no window and the medic did what the contract allowed. The name
  is not carried."*

### 4.5 The Coil

*Fires:* a hull is scrapped or outlawed with a living coil set aboard, and two
or more hulls can reach it within a window.

*Sable* is outlawed — her hull refused a judgment on a killing and would not
pay the fifty thousand hours — and she is three windows out with the polity's
second-last coil set aboard. *Meridian* could take her at the cost of its
window, or the new-built hull that has no coil at all. The hull-master says
take it: a coil is a life for thirty sailors. The Voided says an outlawed hull
is still a hull, and if the polity takes coils by force there will be no coils
that are not taken by force. The Ledger says offer *Sable* the judgment
commuted to the coil.

- **Commute the blood-price to the coil.** Sable pays with her coil and sails
  thin; outlawry lifts; the victim's kin get hours from the new-built hull.
  *"Sable gave her coil for the dead and sails thin. The Keep calls it justice
  and the Thin call it a price."*
- **Seize it.** A boarding; a skill test; casualties drawn; the coil moves;
  every hull knows the polity takes property by force. *"They took Sable's
  coil at the Lean with two dead. It is cold now in a hull that did not earn
  it."*
- **Let her go.** Sable takes the coil to another clan; the polity is down to
  one; the Thin's future shortens by a generation. *"Sable went out with her
  coil to the garrison and we did not follow."*
- **Open the boron.** If stockpiled: begin the MgB₂ rewind for the new hull
  instead; a decade of work; the coil argument becomes a capability argument.
  *"The ring opened the boron. It is a Fortune of drawing wire before anyone
  knows if it holds."*

### 4.6 The Box

*Fires:* locally born adults ≥ 50% of adults; the polity has no self-name in
its lexicon state.

The Rememberer-elect has the Consortium's last personnel roll, and every child
in the Keep is on it under a two-letter code — *VB*, the box for dependants
born in situ with no return allocation — which the rotators said as *veeb* and
meant as an insult, and the young say to each other. The moot must decide what
the polity is called and, more, against whom (07 §3.8): the Office, the
garrison, the Far-Wet, or the Kept and Thin against each other. The Unkept say
the word is theirs, since only their children were born in the void. The
Keep-mother says a name that belongs to one hull is not a name.

- **Voidborn, against the Office.** The Silence seat stays empty; the act-3
  negotiating posture hardens. *"At the eighth Fortune we took the Office's
  box and made it a name."*
- **Voidborn, against the garrison.** Garrison relations fall; inner-system
  posture softens. *"We are the Voidborn and the Dry are the Office's
  leftovers, and we said so at the moot."*
- **Kept and Thin, two names.** Divergence accelerates; ring seats begin to
  split. *"The Keep wrote Kept on the roll and the hulls wrote Thin, and
  nobody wrote the box word."*
- **Refuse the box; take the rock's name.** *Fortunate*; the Unkept dissent
  and keep *Voidborn* for themselves. *"We are the Fortunate, the Rememberer
  said, and the Unkept laughed and kept the other word."*

## 5. Advisor voices, year 30

*The question:* Mars offers helium and boron — enough to keep the coils cold
and, in time, rewind one — for a Martian transponder on every hull and a
Martian inspector resident in the Keep.

**Okonkwo, who answers for the children (Kept).** "Take it. The coils are our
sons' lives, and I have counted arms at the lock for twenty Fortunes. But on
his first light the inspector will count the children, ask which were born
aboard, and have a word for the Unkept that we do not use. Take the helium.
Give him the census. Do not let him into the bone ledger, and do not let him
see the quiet room. And when he asks who is human, send him to Haddad."

**Varga, who answers for the hulls (Thin).** "A transponder is a leash with a
light on it. The Dry would put one on us for nothing; the near well at least
pays. Here is the lay: we register four hulls and we keep two that Mars has
never seen, and those two never come in while the inspector is at the lock. If
the ring cannot promise me the two, then no — we phase free on the sails, we
go out thin, and I would rather my daughters count seven marks and stop than
have every window of their lives written in a Martian book."

**Haddad, who answers for the sky (Voided).** "I have seen the near well. It
is a coin at arm's length; it has no registry that I could see from there. Ask
what Mars fears — not us; the garrison's server, and the Metal's chips going
to someone else — and make them bid against the Dry. Take the helium at the
Loud, when the Sun is bad and they want our water most. As for the inspector:
seat him in the Silence. Let him learn what that chair is for."

**Reyes, senior Hand of Uncle.** "The helium is for Uncle too; his coolers are
the same machines as the coils'. So yes, and quickly. But an inspector will
ask for the logs, and there are no logs since the second convoy — that is the
crime in their book, not the aunts. The transponders are radios and the aunts
are inside the line. An aunt can tell a transponder where a hull is. She can
also tell it where a hull is not. Take the deal and let the Hands write the
transponders' lay. If the inspector asks to see Uncle, he sees the farm loop.
He does not see the chronicle."

**Tanaka, who answers for the hours (Ledger).** "The numbers. One coil set is
thirty sailors at forty years instead of two: a thousand sailor-years, and a
sailor-year is two and a half thousand hours of wet and metal coming in. The
inspector costs perhaps three hundred hours a Fortune of showing him things;
the transponders nothing. The helium is cheaper than the alternative by a
hundred to one. Take it — and write it as a lay with a term, so that at the
next Mars-turn we are renegotiating fractions and not begging. Never sign
anything that does not end at a window."

## 7. Names

**Personal names: three, and a rule for each.**

1. *The carried name.* A child is given the name of someone planted since the
   last birth, in planting order; the Keep-mother keeps the list. When the
   pool is short the names take ordinals — *Ines-the-third*, *Second Amadi*.
   The pool is small and recycled, as Tristan's seven surnames are (07 §1.4):
   Amadi, Ines, Kofi, Lind, Mira, Tomas, Weight (the drum's last child), Yara.
2. *The watch-name.* Earned aboard or in the farm from an incident, and used
   as the adult name: *Halfwet*, *Twice-Quenched*, *Sable's daughter*,
   *Steamed*, *Milk*, *Clip*; farm names *Lean-born*, *Tile*.
3. *The share.* Whose hull or Keep: *of Meridian*, *of the Keep*, *of the
   Unkept*. The sponsor's surnames survive only as clipped *lineage* words
   (*Kwo*, *Varg*, *Lind*, *Rey*, *Tan*, *Had*) the Keep-mother uses to
   enforce exogamy.

So: *Ines-the-third Halfwet of Meridian, a Lind.* A Voided drops the share —
*Haddad, Voided* — because she has none.

**Ship names.** The registry name (*FCV-03 Meridian*) is clipped by the crew
(*Merry*) and becomes the mind's kin-name (*Aunt Merry*). Rule: **a hull is
named for its mind; a mind is named by its crew; a hull without a mind is
named for what it lost** (*Late-Merry*, *Sable's hull*). A new-built hull is
nameless until it has a mind or a Voided names it at launch (*Haddad's hull*).
Belt-made tray-minds are named as children: *Merry's child*.

**Rock names.** The catalogue name is the Office's word (*19 Fortuna*, *Site
F*). Rule: **a rock is its volatiles plus its phase**, using the fixed
co-orbital geometry of report 01: *the Keep*, *the Dry* (an S-type), *the
Metal* (an M-type), *Far-Wet* (the Themis water), *Downstream Thisbe*,
*Upstream Pallas* (the mountain nobody reaches).

**Ring seats.** A seat is a question — *who answers for the children, the
hulls, the hours, the air, the Hands, the sky*, and *the Silence* — and the
holder is whoever the ring accepts the answer from. Rule: **when the polity
starts asking a new question, a seat appears.** *Who answers for the Unkept*
is the seat the player will watch form; *who answers for the human* is the one
the treaty will demand.
